Foundation and Base Plates

A stiffened slab on 4' of select fill, with grade beams under every wall and frame line and a square footing under every steel column and post. Each of the 45 steel members that touches the ground gets a base plate with anchor rods cast into its footing, and the 13 main frame columns also sit on drilled concrete piers that reach below the active clay. Why this foundation

  • The SoilsTEC report found very high swell clay (PI up to 82). Left alone it can heave about 3.3". Replacing the top 4' with compacted select fill brings that down to about 1".
  • Grade beams under every wall and across the house on every revised frame line (13.5'–20' apart) keep the slab stiff. PE-directed Grid 2 is at x=13.5', with its rear column on the y=41.5' main wall; Grid 6 is at x=78', with its rear support at y=36.9' on the Great Room wall. Recheck the 4'7" rear rafter cantilever, beam/footing load path and plumbing sleeve before construction.
  • The steel frames push out about 4 k at each base under roof load and pull up as much as 8.3 k in a 140 mph wind. The footings are heavy enough to hold them down, and the frame-line beams tie each pair of columns together.
  • Each main frame column also sits on an 18" drilled pier, 15' long, that reaches below the active clay. The slab can still move a little with the soil, but the steel frames stay put, and a pier is far cheaper to install now than to add under a finished house. Piers under the porch and suite columns can be added the same way if the PE wants them.

Build sequence and tests

  • Strip vegetation, topsoil, roots and organic material. Set up positive drainage away from the pad.
  • Proof-roll the subgrade to 95% of maximum dry density (ASTM D698) at -2% to +3% of optimum moisture, with a 25-ton pneumatic roller or a loaded dump truck. Undercut soft or wet zones and backfill in 6" compacted lifts. Do not let the exposed subgrade dry out before fill goes on.
  • Place select fill (LL 35 or less, PI 10-20) in 8" loose lifts (about 6" compacted) at 95% D698, -2% to +3% of optimum, 5' past the slab. SoilsTEC density-tests and approves every lift before the next: at least one test per 2,500 sf per lift, about 4 per lift and 32 in all for 4' of fill.
  • Drill the 13 piers (P1) through the accepted pad. SoilsTEC or the PE checks the depth and a clean bottom, the cage goes in with its bars reaching into the F1 cap, and the concrete is placed the same day with no standing water. Spread the spoils (about 17 CY of native clay) away from the house.
  • Dig the beams and footings as soon as the pad is accepted and pour soon after; a long-open excavation weakens the bearing soil. Set vapor retarder, rebar and the anchor-rod templates. Survey every rod group.
  • Plumbing rough-in per plan sheet P-101: drains clear of beams and footings, horizontal sleeves through beams, collars at every slab exit, concrete cut-off collars or clay plugs where a utility trench crosses the building line. Survey the rough-in with the anchor rods.
  • Propane: trench the PE line from the tank pad to the riser outside the suite east wall, 18" deep with tracer wire, clear of the F2 footings, piers and the drain lines. No gas pipe goes under the slab. The propane contractor sets the tank, tests the line and approves the riser before backfill.
  • Before the pour SoilsTEC (or its representative) approves the bearing level: clear of loose soil, standing water and debris. Never pour on soil softened by rain or seepage; remove and replace it. The PE or county inspection follows.
  • Pick the pour date with rainfall in mind: a slab placed at the end of a wet period loses perimeter support as the soil dries; one placed after a long dry spell sees edge uplift when the rains return.
  • Pour beams, footings and slab together. Cure 7 days before setting steel; grout under plates after the frames are plumbed.
  • Grade 5% away from the slab for 10' (report minimum 3%); gutters and downspouts discharge 10'+ away.

Drainage, planting and upkeep

The PVR values in the report hold only while the site stays drained. If water ponds or the wetted front reaches deeper, the report warns movement can run 2 to 3 times higher. These are owner duties for the life of the house.

  • Keep final grade within 10 ft of the house sloping away, 3% at least (the plan uses 5%). Walks, drives and flatwork within 10 ft slope away too.
  • Seal the joint between the house and any pavement or sidewalk. Walk the joints each year and re-seal where needed. Plan for flatwork to move a little after construction.
  • Gutters and downspouts carry roof water 10 ft or more from the house. Roof drains discharge onto pavement or are extended away, ideally to a closed pipe.
  • Plant no large trees or shrubs closer than roughly their mature height. Keep beds next to the slab from staying wet; use self-contained planters. Run sprinkler mains at least 5 ft from the house.
  • Hold moisture steady around the slab edge: no ponding in planters, bare ground or paving joints. Fix plumbing and irrigation leaks promptly.
