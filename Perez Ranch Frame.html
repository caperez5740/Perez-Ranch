    Where wood goes

    Exterior walls below the 12' eave, between the steel columns: 2x6 Douglas fir #2 at 16" o.c. (don't substitute SPF or pine at 16"). Bottom plate: ground-contact treated 2x6, anchored to the slab. Top plate: double 2x6, clipped to the steel eave strut. Openings: 2x6 king + jack studs, 2-ply 2x12 headers, 2x6 sills. Sheathing: 7/16" OSB + taped house wrap under the 26 ga panels, and a 4'-0" limestone wainscot below them. Interior walls (shown with "Wood only"): 2x4 SPF at 16" o.c., non-bearing, treated bottom plate, double top plate. Main-house partitions are 10' tall; Mom's suite partitions are 8'. Stained beams: exposed 8x10 beams under the stand-alone vaults of the great room and master bedroom (ridge beam, edge beam, two cross beams each). Every door opening is framed 3'-2" wide for a 36" door (wheelchair access), with 2x4 king + jack studs and a flat 2x4 header Everything else (frames, posts, purlins, eave struts, gable girts, porches, carport) stays steel.

    Attic space above the 10' ceilings

    The Wood only (exterior and interior wood framing) and Attic space buttons can be combined; tap again to turn one off. Tap Attic space. Flat ceilings are 10' in the house (bedroom 2, foyer and office included), carport and breezeway, and 8' in Mom's suite and on her porch; the master bedroom has a stand-alone vault (10' at the east and west walls rising to 12' at the ridge) with attic above it, and the great room stays vaulted. With a flat ceiling under the foamed roof: dark green = room for an 8' ceiling (about 1,000 sq ft); medium green = a 7' ceiling fits (about 255 sq ft more, 1,255 total); light green = 5'–7', storage or sloped ceiling to a knee wall. Main attic (floor at about 11'): one continuous space from the east gable over the carport, breezeway, master bath, master bedroom, closets and kitchen, about 890 sq ft at 8' (a strip about 14' wide, 61' long) or about 1,060 sq ft at 7'. The steel frames at grids 2, 3 and 4 cross it as exposed beams. Front attic (floor at about 11', under the front porch gable roof over bedroom 2, the foyer and the office): about 90 sq ft at 8' and 180 sq ft at 7', highest over the foyer; over bedroom 2 itself it is mostly 5'–7' (storage) because the porch roof slopes down toward its west wall. The great room vault separates it from the main attic, so it needs its own stair or pull-down. Mom's suite attic (orange, floor at about 9' over her 8' ceilings and the 8' porch ceiling, about 400 sq ft at 5'+): her own A/C air handler and water heater, right above her rooms for short ducts and pipes. It is a separate space (the main roof runs under the suite roof) with its own pull-down stair in the suite. Main house mechanical attic (orange, over bedroom 3 and bath 3, about 165 sq ft at 5'+): the main house A/C air handler and water heater on a framed platform, reached by a pull-down stair in the bedroom 3 closet or hall below. Code needs for each: an access opening at least 22" × 30" (big enough for the largest unit), a solid 24"-wide walkway no longer than 20', a 30" × 30" service platform, a light switched at the access and an outlet by the equipment, a drain pan under the water heater and a secondary pan or float switch under the air handler. Study only: needs a structural floor (40–50 psf+, steel floor beams over the carport span), an insulated floor over the open carport, code stairs and the PE's frame check.
